Hi, I'm Mo Fahim.

I've spent more than 25 years building software end to end, taking ideas from design through development, testing, deployment and release across APIs, web applications and mobile platforms. Along the way, I've done most of the jobs that process involves: writing code, designing systems, shaping architecture, and being the person ultimately responsible when something ships. For the last decade, I've focused on leading the teams that do it.

These days, that work looks like this:

- Leading engineering for national industry platforms: web and mobile apps, and the APIs that partner software integrates with
- Shaping the architecture: GraphQL in front of REST services, running on AWS with infrastructure defined as code
- Keeping long-lived platforms healthy: technical audits for end-of-life dependencies, upgrade roadmaps, and a register that puts technical debt in front of the business alongside feature work
- Releasing with less risk: feature flags, CI/CD pipelines, isolated environments, and root cause analysis when something does go wrong
- Turning ideas into plans people can act on: effort estimates, implementation and go-live plans, and regular reporting to stakeholders
- Growing the team's practice: onboarding guides, coding standards, retrospectives, and bringing AI-assisted tooling into everyday engineering work

That experience has taught me less about how technology is supposed to work and more about how it actually behaves once it leaves the whiteboard.

## What I write about

Two things still hold my interest after all these years.

The first is delivery: how to turn ideas into working software, and how to keep that journey short, honest, and free from ceremony that adds more friction than value.

The second is people. What makes a group of engineers work well together? How is culture built, maintained, or quietly lost? And what does effective leadership look like when the people around you often know more than you do about the problem directly in front of them?

This blog started in 2015 as notes to myself on software development. These days I write about delivery and people at [Forever Draft](https://foreverdraft.substack.com): mostly short notes drawn from real projects, outages, decisions and mistakes, with the occasional longer piece.

Nothing here is presented as the final word. Much of it may turn out to be poorly thought out, wrong or just plain nonsense. These are snapshots of what I was thinking at the time, shared in public and open to revision. After all, the best ideas are usually still drafts.

If you've seen things differently, I'd love to hear about it: mfahim[at]gmail[dot]com.
